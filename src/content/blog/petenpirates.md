Ever since I switched from Spotify to Youtube Music, I've found myself more apprecitvie of its clever autoplays and mixes.<br />
I found many of my favourites on a random day's listen while keeping YTM doing its thing in the background, and finding Pete and The Pirates was a similar discovery.

I was in a car ride to a doctor appointment one morning and I remember hearing Blood Gets Thin out of nowhere, and it really roared in my ear. It got me wide-eyed, “What did I stumble into?”

<h2> Blood Gets Thin: </h2>
 This song is one of those you don't really know what's going to happen next in it, it's just a surprise!<br />
It really caught me on the first listen purely instrumentally, the bass and the guitar really really complete each other and both of them force you to notice how they shine, especially on every break between verses. <br />
The singer is also vocally very forward that he's got what to say, his aggression loud and for it to be the first song to hear from him, it grabs you by your collar and you're confused, where did that hand come from? <br /><br />

<span class="lyric-trigger" data-song="song-blood" data-verse="blood-intro"><em>“Here she comes, she's cool like sin<br />
I can feel my blood get thin<br />
Here she comes, she'll suck me in<br />
Take a deep breath and begin”</em></span><br />

Immediately thrown into a narrative you know nothing about, but you can tell the narrator is a troubled man.
Either she's a woman he fancies, or a metaphor to something he can't escape, he's a goner, and not only he knows it, but he braces himself to face it. 

<span class="lyric-trigger" data-song="song-blood" data-verse="blood-wild"><em>“She's wild-eyed and confident<br />
Heard my words, knew what I meant<br />
You could be my only friend<br />
When they dump me in the end<br />
It isn't money why she sticks around<br />
She's stuck in mud, she's trying to take me down<br />
She behaves like she's on fire<br />
On her tip-toes, reach up higher”</em></span><br />

Delving further, it sounds more and more like a parasitic relationship where she's basically feeding off him. She's wild and equally attractive, not necessarily because she's beautiful, but she's attracting him, pulling him into madness with her. She's trouble and if she's going down, she wants to take him down with her.<br><br>
A relationship clearly destructive! <br><br>
<span class="lyric-trigger" data-song="song-blood" data-verse="blood-doctor"><em>“And if the doctor can't do tricks<br />
You can use your normal fix”</em></span> helps it lean more into the interpretation she's but a personification of addiction, likely to drug abuse, something he can't rid himself off easily, or simply an addict pulling a guy to fall into her same very mistakes and join her in hell, where she can't escape nor he can now. 
She's created herself company in misery, not exactly a lover like he deems her.

Then the second verse kicks you because you've been driven a certain beat of chaos and sung misery, and suddenly you realise something... 

<span class="lyric-trigger" data-song="song-blood" data-verse="blood-wake"><em>“And I can't wake her up, because she's already dead”</em></span><br />
Wait, what? <br />
<span class="lyric-trigger" data-song="song-blood" data-verse="blood-wake"><em>“I can't wake her up; did you hear what I said?”</em></span><br />

Did she OD? Was she ever alive? Has he just sobered up to a reality he was escaping: that she's a dead woman all along? Or is she even real? 

You're not given the proper chance to overthink what he just said because the guitar slices the momentary silence and so joins in the bass for a chorus appearance once more.

I like to think she's indeed an addict dragging a man to her level because she can't get better herself, and gets him hooked on the same trouble she's in. While he? He's doing it all to be as cool as she is, but maybe she's cool because she's been dead, maybe he's abusing drugs even more to forget about it! 

There's also this interpretation that she's a vampire, which fits a lot of the lines, especially how she feeds off him, and makes his blood run thin, how she's <em>cool</em> and <em>wild-eyed</em>.<br />
A fun imagery and a cool song! 


<h2>Come To The Bar:</h2>

This really sounds like a drunken rumble about stagnation and nostalgia to me  
Almost 6 minutes of a man sick of everything and deciding to have fun forever, and drink himself for the fun of it.

But there's more background to it, where throughout the entire song he's addressing a friend that's not around anymore, lyrics hinting at a rough break up between him and the rest of the friends group, but now hoping he'd come back to town, drink and dance with them like they used to. 

<span class="lyric-trigger" data-song="song-bar" data-verse="bar-blackbird"><em>“Hi there Mr Blackbird, your friends are calling you<br />
So you better fly up high in the cherry tree<br />
But keep one little eye fixed on your girlfriend<br />
Or she will come sit on my window and talk to me”</em></span><br /><br>
I really like how he described all of them as birds xd it's not a regular tool, but it sounds silly where it is, and that's not unusual coming from a babbling drunk.
I like this verse for one more other reason: How he's urging the friend to come back using two methods.<br><br> 
<strong>1- Endearment:</strong> He's being sweet, urging and pulling him back with that "Come on!" tone, calling him names—where "Blackbird" could refer to something physical like dark hair, or serve as a nickname like "Gloomy Face." He's just being a joker: a funny, tipsy man nudging and sweet-talking you into coming back to town.<br>

I've Looked further into the symbolism behind choosing blackbirds specifically, and they’re seen as good omens of positive change and transformation! Is it possible he sees in his friend’s arrival in town again a sign of life turning back to good once more?<br><br>

And, is that a blackbird?<br><br>
<div style="text-align: center;">
<img src="https://t2.genius.com/unsafe/258x258/https%3A%2F%2Fimages.genius.com%2Fd474cc033b12ea62001acace3e68ef74.500x500x1.webp" /> </div>

<strong> 2- Threatening:</strong> when being nice doesn't seem to work, he's got a winning card in threats, he thinks!<br> He's telling him he's got to come back to keep an eye or his girlfriend might have to seek someone better.<br> A sly remark, a shit-eating grin to match even, a smirk enough to get that friend’s blood boiling with jealousy and maybe the way to get him back.  

Then chorus, which says all that again but in plain words, and more pleas<br />
<span class="lyric-trigger" data-song="song-bar" data-verse="bar-croc"><em>“It doesn't matter what happened to you, you're my crocodile<br />
It doesn't matter, doesn't matter at all, just come to the bar<br />
Get back into the rhythm of things and come to the bar”</em></span><br />

Crocodile, digging into the bri’ish slang where the band is from, means someone who's tough, got hard skin, and someone's who's been through it and made it out.<br>
So I see this as a way he's trying to convince him that it doesn't matter what happened to you, what happened between us, because no matter how badly things ended, you still have a place here, so come home, celebrate with us! 

Just come to the bar, man! 

Then we have this hit of nostalgia, somewhere back in time in good ol’ 1979 and Blondie's "Heart of Glass” is playing in the background. <br>
That girl's got her fun man, a bird that is lively, a real dancer. <br>
He's got good dancing shoes (Even saying he’s well-dressed), and his fingers are sticky from the alcohol, probably a nod at the amount of cheers he said and pints he clanked?  <br>
He was the soul of the party, the one to dance all night till the carpet wore out. <br><br>

Then <span class="lyric-trigger" data-song="song-bar" data-verse="bar-repeat">the chorus repeats on and on</span> without an answer, an emphasis on how the way things got ugly between them doesn't matter at all, he's just to come back and forget it all happened.<br> 
But maybe a descent into how it doesn't matter if he returns or not? 

He just wants the good old days back, to get back on the rhythm they once shared, and for him to come back to the bar.<br>
Especially as the sound starts losing focus like someone drowning (literally in alcohol), spacing out, nothing he hears well, he's drunk as a skunk. 

Instrumentally it's linear, but it's really really fun, really dancy synths and makes you one want to move and just have a little carefree fun!<br>
The bass is <em>wonderfully</em> driving it all in the background and the drums go crazy in tune.
Vocally I indeed now see well how much Thomas Sanders sounds like Tylor Jospeh, but in his own way.

Finally, it's safe to say that not only Teleman and Pete and The Pirates share the same band members, but I do highly think they carried the sound with them to Teleman's production, although sometimes there's songs such as <em>Repeater</em> that sounds straight up like an unreleased P&TP song.<br><br>

A fun band that will always be cherished in my playlists.
